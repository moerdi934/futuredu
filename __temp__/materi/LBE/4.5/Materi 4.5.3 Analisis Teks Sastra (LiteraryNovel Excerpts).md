# SECTION 4: ANALISIS TEKS KOMPLEKS

## Topik 4.5: Genre-Specific Text Analysis


### Materi 4.5.3: Analisis Teks Sastra (Literary/Novel Excerpts)

---

**Welcome to the World of Fiction! 📚✨**

Teks Sastra itu basically **mini-movie di kepala kamu**. Ada tokoh, ada konflik, ada emosi, dan bahasa yang bikin kamu bisa **ngebayangin** scene-nya dengan jelas. Ini genre yang paling **artistic** dan butuh **imagination** untuk fully appreciate!

---

**🎬 Literary Elements: The Building Blocks of Story**

Setiap teks sastra punya **7 elemen fundamental**:

---

**ELEMENT #1: CHARACTER (Tokoh)**

**Types:**
- **Protagonist:** main character (biasanya "good guy", tapi not always!)
- **Antagonist:** karakter yang opposed ke protagonist (bisa villain, bisa circumstance)
- **Supporting characters:** teman, keluarga, mentor, dll
- **Flat vs Round:** flat = simple, one-dimensional; round = complex, multi-dimensional
- **Static vs Dynamic:** static = tidak berubah; dynamic = berkembang sepanjang cerita

**What to Look For:**
- **Physical traits:** how they look
- **Personality traits:** how they act, think, feel
- **Motivations:** what drives them?
- **Relationships:** how they interact dengan others
- **Development:** do they change? how?

**Character Revelation Methods:**
- **Direct characterization:** penulis langsung tell (e.g., "She was brave")
- **Indirect characterization:** show through actions, thoughts, dialogue, appearance, others' reactions (STEAL method)

---

**ELEMENT #2: PLOT (Alur)**

**Classic Plot Structure:**

1. **Exposition** (Pengenalan)
   - Setting & characters introduced
   - Background information
   - Initial situation

2. **Rising Action** (Komplikasi)
   - Conflict develops
   - Tension builds
   - Events lead toward climax

3. **Climax** (Puncak Konflik)
   - Turning point
   - Highest tension
   - Most exciting part

4. **Falling Action** (Penurunan)
   - After climax
   - Consequences unfold
   - Moving toward resolution

5. **Resolution/Denouement** (Penyelesaian)
   - Conflict resolved
   - Loose ends tied up
   - New equilibrium

**Dalam SNBT excerpt:** biasanya dapat **sebagian** dari plot, not complete arc. Kamu harus identify **which part** you're reading!

---

**ELEMENT #3: SETTING (Latar)**

**Components:**
- **Place:** where the story happens (house, city, country, planet!)
- **Time:** when it happens (era, season, time of day)
- **Social context:** culture, social norms, economic condition

**Why Setting Matters:**
- Creates **atmosphere/mood** (dark forest = suspense; beach = relaxed)
- Influences **character behavior** (Victorian era vs modern)
- Can be **symbolic** (storm = turmoil)
- Sometimes acts as **antagonist** (man vs nature)

**How Setting is Described:**
- Sensory details: visual, auditory, olfactory, tactile
- Descriptive language: adjectives, imagery
- Character's perception: how they experience the place

---

**ELEMENT #4: THEME (Tema)**

**Definition:**  
Central idea or message tentang life/human nature yang explored dalam story.

**Common Themes:**
- 💔 **Love & relationships:** family, friendship, romance
- ⚔️ **Good vs Evil:** moral conflicts
- 🎭 **Identity & self-discovery:** who am I?
- 💀 **Life & death:** mortality, legacy
- 🏛️ **Power & corruption:** abuse of authority
- 🌱 **Growing up/Coming of age:** maturity
- ⚖️ **Justice & injustice:** fairness, equality
- 🆓 **Freedom vs oppression:** liberty, control
- 🌍 **Man vs society:** individual vs collective

**Identifying Theme:**
- NOT the same as topic/subject (Love adalah topic, "Love conquers all" adalah theme)
- Expressed as **complete idea/statement**
- **Universal**—applicable beyond the specific story
- Often **implicit**, not stated directly
- Look for: repeated ideas, symbol meanings, character changes, conflicts

---

**ELEMENT #5: POINT OF VIEW (Sudut Pandang)**

**Types:**

**First Person (Aku/Saya):**
- Uses: "I", "me", "we", "us"
- Narrator is a character in the story
- **Limited:** hanya tahu thoughts & feelings si narrator
- **Intimate:** close to narrator's experience
- Example: *"I walked into the room, my heart pounding."*

**Second Person (Kamu):**
- Uses: "you"
- Rare in fiction, more in self-help
- Reader becomes character
- Example: *"You open the door and see..."*

**Third Person Limited:**
- Uses: "he", "she", "they"
- Narrator outside story, follows ONE character
- Knows that character's thoughts only
- Example: *"She wondered if anyone would notice."*

**Third Person Omniscient:**
- Uses: "he", "she", "they"
- Narrator knows EVERYTHING
- All characters' thoughts, feelings, future, past
- Example: *"While Sarah smiled, she felt sad. Across the room, John sensed something was wrong."*

**Why POV Matters:**
- Determines **what information** reader gets
- Creates **sympathy/distance** with characters
- Affects **reliability** (first person could be biased!)
- Influences **tone** of story

---

**ELEMENT #6: CONFLICT (Konflik)**

**Types:**

**Internal Conflict (Man vs Self):**
- Struggle **within** character
- Emotions, decisions, moral dilemmas
- Example: "Should I tell the truth and hurt her?"

**External Conflicts:**

1. **Man vs Man:**
   - Character vs another character
   - Most common
   - Example: Hero vs Villain

2. **Man vs Society:**
   - Character vs social norms, laws, culture
   - Example: Fighting against injustice

3. **Man vs Nature:**
   - Character vs natural forces
   - Example: Surviving a storm

4. **Man vs Technology:**
   - Character vs machines, AI
   - Example: Robots taking over

5. **Man vs Supernatural:**
   - Character vs ghosts, gods, fate
   - Example: Cursed by a witch

**Identifying Conflict:**
- What is the **main problem**?
- Who/what is **opposing** the protagonist?
- What is **at stake**?

---

**ELEMENT #7: TONE & MOOD**

**Tone:**
- Author's **attitude** toward subject/characters
- Examples: humorous, serious, sarcastic, melancholic, bitter, affectionate

**Mood:**
- **Atmosphere** created for reader
- How the text makes YOU feel
- Examples: suspenseful, gloomy, cheerful, tense, romantic, eerie

**How to Identify:**
- **Word choice (diction):** dark words = dark mood
- **Sentence structure:** short = tension; long = leisurely
- **Imagery:** what pictures are created?
- **Sound devices:** rhythm, alliteration affect mood

---

**🎨 Language Features: The Art of Literary Writing**

---

**FEATURE #1: DESCRIPTIVE LANGUAGE**

**Purpose:** Paint vivid pictures, immerse reader in the world

**Techniques:**

**A. Sensory Details** (Appeal to 5 senses)

- **Visual:** colors, shapes, sizes, appearance
  - *"The crimson sun melted into the horizon"*

- **Auditory:** sounds, volume, tone
  - *"The clock ticked loudly in the silent room"*

- **Olfactory:** smells
  - *"The scent of fresh bread wafted through the air"*

- **Tactile:** textures, temperatures
  - *"The rough bark scratched against her palm"*

- **Gustatory:** tastes
  - *"The bitter coffee burned her tongue"*

**B. Adjectives & Adverbs** (Describing words)

- Not just "house" → "dilapidated, creaky mansion"
- Not just "walked" → "strolled leisurely" or "stumbled wearily"

**C. Precise Nouns & Verbs**

- "He ran" → "He sprinted" / "He dashed" / "He bolted"
- Different verbs create different images!

---

**FEATURE #2: FIGURATIVE LANGUAGE**

Bahasa **non-literal** untuk create deeper meaning:

---

**A. METAPHOR**

**Definition:** Direct comparison tanpa "like" atau "as"

**Formula:** X **is** Y

**Examples:**
- "Time is money" (time = money)
- "Her eyes were diamonds" (eyes = diamonds)
- "The world is a stage" (world = stage)

**Purpose:**
- Make abstract → concrete
- Create powerful imagery
- Convey complex ideas simply

**SNBT Tip:** Kalau ada metaphor, jangan artikan **literally**! Cari **implied meaning**-nya.

---

**B. SIMILE**

**Definition:** Comparison using "like" atau "as"

**Formula:** X is **like/as** Y

**Examples:**
- "She swims like a fish" (grace in water)
- "He is as brave as a lion" (very brave)
- "The stars shone like diamonds" (bright, sparkly)

**Purpose:**
- Make description more vivid
- Easier to understand than metaphor
- Create relatable comparisons

---

**C. PERSONIFICATION**

**Definition:** Giving human qualities to non-human things

**Examples:**
- "The wind **whispered** through the trees"
- "The sun **smiled** down on us"
- "Death **knocked** on his door"
- "The flowers **danced** in the breeze"

**Purpose:**
- Make inanimate things relatable
- Create vivid imagery
- Convey emotions

---

**D. HYPERBOLE**

**Definition:** Extreme exaggeration for effect

**Examples:**
- "I'm so hungry I could eat a horse"
- "I've told you a million times"
- "This bag weighs a ton"

**Purpose:**
- Emphasize feelings
- Create humor
- Make point memorable

**SNBT Tip:** Recognize sebagai exaggeration, don't take literally!

---

**E. IMAGERY**

**Definition:** Vivid descriptive language that appeals to senses

**Example:**
> *"The old house stood alone on the hill, its broken windows gaping like empty eye sockets. The wind moaned through the cracks, and the smell of decay hung heavy in the air."*

**Creates:** Visual (broken windows), auditory (wind moaning), olfactory (smell of decay) imagery

**Purpose:**
- Immerse reader in scene
- Make experience vivid
- Evoke emotions

---

**F. SYMBOLISM**

**Definition:** Object/character/event represents abstract idea

**Common Symbols:**
- 🕊️ **Dove** = peace
- 💀 **Skull** = death/mortality
- 🌹 **Rose** = love/beauty
- 🌑 **Darkness** = evil/unknown
- ☀️ **Light** = hope/knowledge
- 🌊 **Water** = life/cleansing/emotion
- 🔥 **Fire** = passion/destruction/transformation

**In Stories:**
- **Colors:** red = passion/danger, white = purity, black = evil
- **Weather:** storm = turmoil, sunshine = happiness
- **Objects:** mirror = self-reflection, door = opportunity/transition

**SNBT Tip:** If something is **described in detail** or **mentioned repeatedly**, it's likely **symbolic**!

---

**🎭 Narrative Techniques: How Story is Told**

---

**TECHNIQUE #1: DIALOGUE**

**Definition:** Conversation between characters

**Formats:**
- Direct: *"I can't believe you did that," she said.*
- Indirect: *She told him she couldn't believe he did that.*

**What Dialogue Reveals:**
- Character **personality** (word choice, manner of speaking)
- **Relationships** (formal vs casual)
- **Conflict** (disagreements, tensions)
- **Information** (advancing plot)
- **Emotions** (through what's said AND how)

**Reading Dialogue:**
- Pay attention to **tone** (angry? sad? sarcastic?)
- Notice **dialogue tags** (whispered, shouted, muttered)
- Look for **subtext** (what's NOT said but implied)

**Example:**
> *"Fine. Do whatever you want," she said quietly, turning away.*

Subtext: She's NOT fine, she's hurt!

---

**TECHNIQUE #2: FLASHBACK**

**Definition:** Scene from the past interrupting present narrative

**Signals:**
- Time shift words: "Ten years ago...", "She remembered when..."
- Past perfect tense: "had done", "had been"
- Memory triggers: smell, sound, object

**Purpose:**
- Provide **backstory**
- Explain **character motivation**
- Add **depth** to current situation
- Create **context**

**SNBT Tip:** Recognize when you're in flashback! Tentukan which events are **past** vs **present**.

---

**TECHNIQUE #3: FORESHADOWING**

**Definition:** Hints about future events

**Examples:**
- Ominous descriptions: *"Little did she know, this was the last time she'd see him."*
- Symbolic warnings: storm clouds before disaster
- Character warnings: "I have a bad feeling about this"
- Objects: gun mentioned in chapter 1, used in chapter 5

**Purpose:**
- Create **suspense**
- Build **anticipation**
- Make plot feel **inevitable** (not random)

**SNBT Tip:** Details that seem **random** or **overly emphasized** might be foreshadowing!

---

**TECHNIQUE #4: IRONY**

**Types:**

**Verbal Irony:**
- Saying **opposite** of what you mean (sarcasm)
- *"Oh great, another rainy day"* (when you hate rain)

**Situational Irony:**
- What happens is **opposite** of what's expected
- Fire station burns down
- Marriage counselor gets divorced

**Dramatic Irony:**
- **Reader knows** something **character doesn't**
- Audience knows killer is in the house, character doesn't
- Creates suspense!

**Purpose:**
- Create **humor**
- Add **complexity**
- Make readers feel **superior** (knowing more)

---

**TECHNIQUE #5: STREAM OF CONSCIOUSNESS**

**Definition:** Narrator's thoughts in continuous, unfiltered flow

**Characteristics:**
- No punctuation atau minimal
- Fragmented sentences
- Jumps between ideas
- Like actual thinking process

**Example:**
> *"The rain again it never stops does it remind me of that day when we she said but why did I I should have known better the umbrella where did I..."*

**Purpose:**
- Show **inner turmoil**
- Reveal **authentic thoughts**
- Create **intimacy** with character

**📖 Reading Literature for SNBT: Strategies**

---

**STRATEGY #1: Character Analysis**

When reading, **track characters**:

**Mental Notes:**
- **Who** are the main characters?
- **What** are their traits?
- **Why** do they act this way?
- **How** do they relate to each other?
- **Do they change?** (static vs dynamic)

**Common Questions:**
- "The character can be best described as..."
- "The character's motivation is..."
- "Why does the character do X?"
- "How does character feel about Y?"

**Answering:**
- Use **textual evidence** (what they say, do, think)
- Consider **context** (situation influencing behavior)
- Look for **patterns** (consistent traits)

---

**STRATEGY #2: Understanding Figurative Language**

**When you see metaphor/simile:**

❌ **DON'T:** Take literally!
- "Her eyes were stars" ≠ her eyes are giant gas balls

✅ **DO:** Find implied comparison
- Her eyes were bright, sparkly, beautiful like stars

**Steps:**
1. Identify it's figurative (tidak masuk akal if literal)
2. Find what's being compared
3. Determine quality being highlighted
4. Check options for **closest meaning**

**Common Question Formats:**
- "The phrase '...' suggests that..."
- "The author uses '...' to convey..."
- "The comparison to '...' implies..."

---

**STRATEGY #3: Theme Identification**

**Finding Theme:**

**Step 1:** Identify **repeated ideas**
- Does a concept keep coming up? (freedom, loyalty, courage)

**Step 2:** Look at **character changes**
- What did they learn?
- How did they grow?

**Step 3:** Examine **conflicts & resolutions**
- What was the struggle about?
- How was it resolved?
- What does that say about life?

**Step 4:** Consider **symbols**
- What do they represent?
- How do they relate to theme?

**Theme Questions:**
- "The central theme is..."
- "The passage explores the idea that..."
- "The author's message is..."

**Answering:**
- Choose **universal statement**, not specific plot
- Should apply **beyond this story**
- Often related to **human nature/life lessons**

---

**STRATEGY #4: Tone & Mood Recognition**

**Identifying Tone:**

**Check:**
- **Word choice:** positive, negative, neutral?
- **Sentence structure:** short/choppy = tense; long/flowing = calm
- **Description focus:** beauty or ugliness?
- **Character portrayal:** sympathetic or critical?

**Common Tones in Literature:**
- Melancholic (sad, gloomy)
- Nostalgic (longing for past)
- Ominous (threatening, foreboding)
- Whimsical (playful, fanciful)
- Somber (serious, grave)
- Bitter (resentful)
- Affectionate (loving, warm)

**Mood Questions:**
- "The atmosphere can be described as..."
- "The passage creates a feeling of..."

**Answering:**
- How does the text make YOU feel?
- What emotions does it evoke?
- But base answer on **textual evidence**!

---

**STRATEGY #5: Inference in Literature**

Literary texts are **full of implications**—things not directly stated.

**What to Infer:**

**Character Psychology:**
- Motivations (why they act)
- Feelings (emotional state)
- Relationships (dynamics)
- Backstory (past events)

**Plot Elements:**
- What happened before excerpt
- What will happen after
- Cause-effect relationships

**Symbolic Meanings:**
- What objects represent
- Deeper significance of events

**Themes:**
- Author's message about life
- Commentary on human nature

**Inference Questions:**
- "It can be inferred that the character..."
- "The scene suggests that..."
- "The relationship between X and Y is..."

**Answering:**
- Base on **clues in text**
- Use **logic** (what makes sense?)
- Consider **literary conventions** (patterns)
- Don't over-infer (stay reasonable!)

**⚠️ Common Traps in Teks Sastra**

---

**TRAP #1: The "Literal Interpretation" Trap**

❌ **Wrong:**
Karakter bilang *"I could die of embarrassment"* → jadi dia mati?

✅ **Correct:**
Recognize **hyperbole/figurative language** → dia sangat malu

**Remember:** Literary language often **non-literal**!

---

**TRAP #2: The "Missing Subtext" Trap**

Dialogue often has **hidden meaning**:

**Example:**
> *"Sure, whatever you want," he said coldly, walking away.*

❌ **Surface:** He agrees
✅ **Subtext:** He's angry, doesn't actually agree

**Pay attention to:**
- How something is said (tone)
- Body language described (walking away)
- Context (conflict before this?)

---

**TRAP #3: The "Confusing Character Voice with Author Voice" Trap**

❌ **Wrong:**
Character says something racist → author is racist?

✅ **Correct:**
Character's views ≠ Author's views
- Author might be criticizing that viewpoint
- Showing complexity of human nature

**Always distinguish:**
- What **characters** think/say
- What **author** believes (from overall message)

---

**TRAP #4: The "Symbol Overthinking" Trap**

Not everything is symbolic!

❌ **Over-analysis:**
"The character drinks coffee. Coffee is dark. Dark represents evil. Character is evil!"

✅ **Balanced:**
Sometimes coffee is just... coffee!

**Symbol Checklist:**
- Is it **emphasized** or **repeated**?
- Is it **described in detail**?
- Does it **connect to themes**?
- Is it **traditionally symbolic**?

If yes to several → likely symbolic!

---

**TRAP #5: The "Plot Summary = Theme" Trap**

❌ **Wrong answer:**
"The theme is about a girl who runs away from home"

✅ **Correct answer:**
"The theme is that true freedom comes with responsibility"

**Remember:**
- Plot = **what happens** (story summary)
- Theme = **what it means** (life lesson/message)

---

**TRAP #6: The "Mood vs Tone Confusion" Trap**

**Mood** = reader's feeling  
**Tone** = author's attitude

**Example:** Horror story

**Mood:** scary, suspenseful, tense (how YOU feel)  
**Tone:** mysterious, ominous (how AUTHOR presents it)

They're often similar tapi not always!

**💡 Pro Reading Tips for Teks Sastra**

---

**TIP #1: Visualize as You Read**

Literary texts want you to **see the scene**:
- Picture characters' faces
- Imagine setting details
- Hear dialogue in your mind

This **boosts comprehension** dramatically!

---

**TIP #2: Mark Emotional Language**

Words showing **feelings** are KEY:
- "trembled" = fear
- "beamed" = joy
- "scowled" = anger

Track emotions → understand character → answer correctly!

---

**TIP #3: Pay Attention to Beginnings and Endings**

**Opening:**
- Sets tone
- Introduces conflict
- Hints at theme

**Ending:**
- Reveals character change
- States/implies theme
- Provides resolution

Both are **goldmines** for main idea questions!

---

**TIP #4: Look for Contrasts**

Literature loves **opposites**:
- Light vs dark
- Past vs present
- Rich vs poor
- Hope vs despair

Contrasts **highlight themes** and **create tension**!

---

**TIP #5: Trust Your Emotional Response (But Verify)**

Your **gut feeling** about mood/tone? Usually right!

BUT:
- Check textual evidence
- Make sure it's based on text, not just personal reaction

Balance **intuition** with **analysis**.

---

**TIP #6: The "Cinema" Test**

Imagine it's a **movie scene**:
- What's the lighting? (mood)
- What's the music? (tone)
- How would actors deliver lines? (emotion)
- What's the camera focusing on? (important details)

If helpful, visualize it cinematically!

---

**🎯 Quick Checklist: "Is This Teks Sastra?"**

✅ Ada characters dengan nama/personality  
✅ Ada plot/storyline developing  
✅ Banyak descriptive language  
✅ Lots of figurative language (metaphor, simile)  
✅ Dialogue present  
✅ Creates specific mood/atmosphere  
✅ Focus on human experience/emotions  
✅ Reads like a story/scene  

Kalau mostly YES → It's Teks Sastra!

---

**🌟 The Bottom Line**

**Teks Sastra = A window into human experience through story** 📖✨

**Key Success Factors:**

1. **Read between the lines**
   - Don't just read words, understand implications
   - Look for subtext & symbolism

2. **Engage emotionally**
   - Feel the story (helps comprehension)
   - But answer objectively!

3. **Analyze characters deeply**
   - Motivations, changes, relationships
   - Most questions about them!

4. **Recognize literary devices**
   - Metaphor, simile, irony, etc.
   - Don't interpret literally!

5. **Find the universal theme**
   - What's the bigger message?
   - Applicable beyond this story?

**Final Wisdom:**

Literary texts seem complex, tapi actually they're about **one thing**: **HUMAN EXPERIENCE**.

Focus on:
- **What characters feel** → emotions
- **Why they act** → motivations
- **What they learn** → theme
- **How it's told** → literary techniques

Master these, dan Teks Sastra becomes your **favorite genre**! 🏆

Because ultimately, literature is just **life in beautiful words**. ✨
