# SECTION 4: ANALISIS TEKS KOMPLEKS

## Topik 4.5: Genre-Specific Text Analysis


### Materi 4.5.4: Analisis Teks Saintek (Scientific/Technical)

---

**Welcome to the Lab! 🔬🧪**

Teks Saintek itu basically **"Show me the facts and nothing but the facts!"** Genre ini adalah **complete opposite** dari Teks Sastra—no emotions, no flowery language, just **pure, cold, hard SCIENCE**!

**🧬 Content Focus: What Science Talks About**

---

**CATEGORY #1: SCIENTIFIC CONCEPTS**

**Definition & Explanation of Natural Phenomena:**

- **Biology:** photosynthesis, evolution, genetics, ecosystems
- **Chemistry:** chemical reactions, molecular structures, elements
- **Physics:** motion, energy, forces, quantum mechanics
- **Astronomy:** planets, stars, space phenomena
- **Earth Science:** plate tectonics, weather patterns, rock cycle

**Example Topics:**
- "How do cells produce energy?"
- "What causes earthquakes?"
- "Understanding DNA replication"
- "The life cycle of stars"

**Key Feature:** **Objective explanation** of how/why things work in nature.

---

**CATEGORY #2: RESEARCH FINDINGS**

**Scientific Studies & Their Results:**

**Typical Structure:**
1. **Research question/hypothesis**
   - What did scientists want to find out?

2. **Methodology**
   - How did they conduct the study?
   - What was the sample size?
   - What tools/procedures used?

3. **Results/Findings**
   - What data was collected?
   - What patterns emerged?

4. **Conclusions/Implications**
   - What do the findings mean?
   - How does this advance knowledge?

**Example Topics:**
- "Study reveals new cancer treatment"
- "Research shows impact of sleep on memory"
- "Scientists discover new species in deep ocean"

**CATEGORY #3: TECHNICAL PROCESSES**

**📐 Language Features: The Science Speak**

---

**FEATURE #1: TECHNICAL TERMINOLOGY**

**Definition:** Specialized vocabulary specific to scientific fields

**Characteristics:**

**A. Precise & Specific**

Not "thing" → **organism**  
Not "stuff" → **substance/compound/element**  
Not "grows" → **proliferates/develops/matures**

**B. Often Latin/Greek Roots**

- **Bio** (life) → biology, antibiotic, biodiversity
- **Geo** (earth) → geology, geography, geothermal
- **Photo** (light) → photosynthesis, photon
- **Thermo** (heat) → thermometer, thermoregulation
- **Hydro** (water) → hydration, hydroelectric

**C. Multi-word Technical Terms**

- Not just "change" → **metabolic transformation**
- Not just "dead cells" → **necrotic tissue**
- Not just "hot" → **elevated temperature** or **pyrogenic**

**Understanding Strategy:**

✅ **Break down the word:**
- Photo-synthesis = light + putting together

✅ **Use context clues:**
- Usually defined or explained nearby

✅ **Look for root meanings:**
- Knowledge of prefixes/suffixes helps!

---

**FEATURE #2: OBJECTIVE TONE**

**Definition:** Neutral, factual, impersonal presentation

**Characteristics:**

**A. No Personal Opinions**

❌ "I think this is amazing!"  
✅ "The results indicate..."

❌ "This discovery is super cool!"  
✅ "This finding represents a significant advancement"

**B. Passive Voice Common**

Active: "Scientists conducted the experiment"  
Passive: "The experiment was conducted" ← more common!

**Why?** Focus on **action/result**, not **actor**.

**C. Third Person**

Uses: "the researchers", "the study", "it", "they"  
Avoids: "I", "we", "you"

**D. Hedging Language (for uncertainty)**

- "may indicate"
- "appears to"
- "suggests that"
- "potentially"
- "likely"

**Why?** Science acknowledges **limitations** and **probability**, not absolute certainty.

---

**FEATURE #3: PRECISE DEFINITIONS**

**How Technical Terms Are Defined:**

**Method 1: Direct Definition**

Formula: **X is Y**

Example:
> *"Photosynthesis is the process by which plants convert light energy into chemical energy."*

**Method 2: Appositive**

Formula: **X, [definition], ...**

Example:
> *"Mitochondria, the powerhouse of the cell, produces ATP."*

**Method 3: Relative Clause**

Formula: **X, which/that [definition], ...**

Example:
> *"Neurons, which are nerve cells that transmit signals, form the nervous system."*

**SNBT Tip:** Pay attention to **commas** and **"is"** statements—often signal definitions!

---

**FEATURE #4: DATA & EVIDENCE**

**Types of Data Presented:**

**A. Statistical Data**

- Percentages: "75% of participants"
- Averages: "The mean temperature was 25°C"
- Ranges: "between 10-20 units"
- Ratios: "3:1 ratio"

**B. Measurements**

- Length: meters, millimeters, kilometers
- Weight: grams, kilograms
- Volume: liters, milliliters
- Temperature: Celsius, Fahrenheit, Kelvin
- Time: seconds, hours, years

**C. Comparative Data**

- "higher than"
- "lower than"
- "twice as much"
- "significantly greater"

**D. Experimental Results**

- "The treatment group showed..."
- "In the control group..."
- "The data revealed..."

**Reading Strategy:**

- **Scan for numbers** (they're important!)
- **Note comparisons** (what's being compared?)
- **Understand units** (what's being measured?)

**🏗️ Common Structures: How Science Texts Are Built**

---

**STRUCTURE #1: PROBLEM-SOLUTION**

**Pattern:**

1. **Problem Statement**
   - Issue/challenge presented
   - Why it matters

2. **Background**
   - What's known
   - What's unknown/needed

3. **Proposed Solution(s)**
   - Research approach
   - Method/technique
   - Innovation

4. **Results/Effectiveness**
   - Did it work?
   - How well?

5. **Implications**
   - What does this mean?
   - Future applications?

**Example Flow:**
- Para 1: Antibiotic resistance is a growing threat
- Para 2: Current antibiotics becoming ineffective
- Para 3: Scientists develop new compound
- Para 4: Tests show it kills resistant bacteria
- Para 5: Potential for new treatment class

---

**STRUCTURE #2: CAUSE-EFFECT**

**Pattern:**

1. **Phenomenon/Effect Introduced**
   - What is happening?

2. **Causes Explored**
   - Why does it happen?
   - Multiple factors?

3. **Mechanisms Explained**
   - HOW do causes lead to effects?
   - Process/pathway

4. **Consequences Discussed**
   - What results from this?
   - Short-term and long-term?

**Signal Words:**
- Cause markers: because, due to, as a result of, caused by, stems from
- Effect markers: therefore, thus, consequently, leads to, results in

**Example Flow:**
- Para 1: Coral reefs are dying worldwide
- Para 2: Rising ocean temperatures cause bleaching
- Para 3: Heat stress forces corals to expel algae
- Para 4: Without algae, corals starve and die
- Para 5: This threatens entire marine ecosystems

---

**STRUCTURE #3: PROCESS DESCRIPTION**

**Pattern:**

1. **Process Introduction**
   - What process will be explained?
   - Why is it important?

2. **Initial Stage/Conditions**
   - Starting point
   - Prerequisites

3. **Step-by-Step Progression**
   - Stage 1 → Stage 2 → Stage 3
   - Each step explained

4. **Final Stage/Product**
   - End result
   - What is produced?

5. **Significance/Applications**
   - Why does this matter?
   - How is it used?

**Signal Words:**
- Sequence: first, second, then, next, subsequently, finally
- Time: initially, during, after, following, ultimately

**Example Flow:**
- Para 1: How cells divide through mitosis
- Para 2: Cell prepares (interphase)
- Para 3: Chromosomes condense (prophase)
- Para 4: Chromosomes align (metaphase)
- Para 5: Cell splits into two (telophase/cytokinesis)

---

**STRUCTURE #4: CLASSIFICATION**

**Pattern:**

1. **Category Introduction**
   - What is being classified?
   - Why classify?

2. **Classification Criteria**
   - What's the basis for grouping?
   - What distinguishes types?

3. **Type 1 Description**
   - Characteristics
   - Examples

4. **Type 2 Description**
   - Characteristics
   - Examples

5. **Type 3, etc.**
   - Additional types
   - Comparisons between types

**Signal Words:**
- Types/kinds: can be classified, fall into categories, types include
- Examples: such as, for instance, including, namely

**Example Flow:**
- Para 1: Rocks are classified into three main types
- Para 2: Igneous rocks form from cooled magma
- Para 3: Sedimentary rocks form from compressed sediments
- Para 4: Metamorphic rocks form from heat/pressure transformation
- Para 5: Each type has distinct characteristics and formation

---

**STRUCTURE #5: COMPARE-CONTRAST**

**Pattern:**

1. **Introduction of Items**
   - What's being compared?
   - Why compare them?

2. **Similarities**
   - What do they have in common?

3. **Difference 1**
   - How do they differ in aspect A?

4. **Difference 2**
   - How do they differ in aspect B?

5. **Conclusion**
   - Which is better/more effective?
   - What's the significance?

**Signal Words:**
- Similarity: similarly, likewise, both, also, in the same way
- Difference: however, whereas, in contrast, unlike, on the other hand

**Example Flow:**
- Para 1: Two approaches to treating diabetes
- Para 2: Both aim to regulate blood sugar
- Para 3: Medication manages symptoms
- Para 4: Lifestyle changes address root cause
- Para 5: Combination approach most effective

---

**📊 Data Presence: Reading Numbers and Graphs**

---

**TYPE #1: STATISTICS IN TEXT**

**Common Formats:**

**Percentages:**
> *"Approximately 23% of participants showed improvement"*

**Reading tip:** Pay attention to:
- What's being measured?
- Compared to what?

**Ranges:**
> *"Temperatures ranged from 15°C to 28°C"*

**Reading tip:**
- Minimum and maximum values
- Variability/consistency

**Averages:**
> *"The mean survival rate was 78%"*

**Reading tip:**
- Mean = average
- Median = middle value
- Mode = most common

**Sample Sizes:**
> *"In a study of 500 individuals..."*

**Reading tip:**
- Larger sample = more reliable
- Consider if representative

---

**TYPE #2: EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS**

**Typical Presentation:**

**Control vs Experimental:**
> *"The control group showed 10% improvement while the treatment group showed 45% improvement"*

**Reading tip:**
- Control = baseline (no treatment)
- Experimental = with intervention
- Compare the difference!

**Before-After:**
> *"Blood pressure decreased from 140/90 to 120/80 mmHg"*

**Reading tip:**
- Initial state vs final state
- Magnitude of change

**Correlation:**
> *"There was a strong positive correlation (r=0.85) between exercise and improved mood"*

**Reading tip:**
- Positive correlation = both increase/decrease together
- Negative correlation = one increases, other decreases
- Correlation ≠ causation!

---

**TYPE #3: TABLES (If Present)**

**Reading Strategy:**

1. **Read title/caption**
   - What data is shown?

2. **Check row and column headers**
   - What categories?
   - What's being measured?

3. **Identify units**
   - Grams? Meters? Percentage?

4. **Look for patterns**
   - Highest/lowest values
   - Trends across rows/columns

5. **Make comparisons**
   - Between groups
   - Across conditions

---

**TYPE #4: MEASUREMENTS**

**Common Units:**

**Length:**
- Kilometer (km) → 1000 m
- Meter (m) → base unit
- Centimeter (cm) → 0.01 m
- Millimeter (mm) → 0.001 m
- Micrometer (μm) → 0.000001 m

**Mass:**
- Kilogram (kg) → 1000 g
- Gram (g) → base unit
- Milligram (mg) → 0.001 g

**Volume:**
- Liter (L) → base unit
- Milliliter (mL) → 0.001 L

**Temperature:**
- Celsius (°C) → water freezes at 0°, boils at 100°
- Kelvin (K) → absolute zero scale
- Fahrenheit (°F) → water freezes at 32°, boils at 212°

**SNBT Tip:** Don't need to convert units (usually), just understand **relative sizes** and **what's being measured**!

**🎯 Typical Questions untuk Teks Saintek**

---

**QUESTION TYPE #1: Main Concept/Topic**

*"What is the main topic of the passage?"*  
*"The passage primarily discusses..."*

**Strategy:**

**Focus on:**
- What **scientific phenomenon/concept** is explained?
- What's the **central question** being addressed?

**Common Patterns:**
- "The passage explains how X works"
- "The passage describes the process of X"
- "The passage discusses factors affecting X"

**NOT:**
- Too specific (one detail dari satu paragraf)
- Too general ("science" atau "biology" aja)

---

**QUESTION TYPE #2: Cause-Effect Relationships**

*"According to the passage, what causes X?"*  
*"The result of Y is..."*  
*"Why does Z occur?"*

**Strategy:**

**Look for signal words:**
- Cause: because, due to, caused by, results from, stems from
- Effect: therefore, thus, consequently, leads to, causes, produces

**Trace the chain:**
- A causes B
- B leads to C
- Therefore, A ultimately causes C

**Example:**
- Rising CO₂ → increased greenhouse effect → global warming → ice melting

---

**QUESTION TYPE #3: Process Steps/Sequence**

*"According to the passage, what happens first in the process?"*  
*"Which step follows X?"*  
*"The sequence of events is..."*

**Strategy:**

**Identify stages:**
- Look for numbered steps OR sequence markers
- First, second, then, next, after, finally

**Understand order:**
- What's the starting condition?
- What's the progression?
- What's the end result?

**Verify:**
- Make sure all steps are in logical order

---

**QUESTION TYPE #4: Vocabulary/Technical Terms**

*"The term 'X' refers to..."*  
*"What does 'Y' mean in the context?"*  
*"The word 'Z' is closest in meaning to..."*

**Strategy:**

**For defined terms:**
- Look for direct definitions (X is Y)
- Check nearby explanations

**For context clues:**
- Read surrounding sentences
- Look for synonyms or restatements
- Use root word knowledge (bio-, geo-, therm-, etc.)

**For specialized terms:**
- Break into parts (photo-synthesis)
- Consider function described
- May need general scientific knowledge

---

**QUESTION TYPE #5: Data Interpretation**

*"According to the data, what can be concluded?"*  
*"The results show that..."*  
*"Which statement is supported by the findings?"*

**Strategy:**

**Read data carefully:**
- What was measured?
- What were the results?
- How do groups compare?

**Stick to what's stated:**
- Don't over-interpret
- Don't add information not present
- Focus on direct relationships shown

**Be specific:**
- Note exact values if given
- Identify trends (increase, decrease, stable)
- Recognize significance of differences

---

**QUESTION TYPE #6: Purpose of Information**

*"Why does the author mention X?"*  
*"The example of Y is used to..."*  
*"The data in paragraph 3 serves to..."*

**Strategy:**

**Common purposes:**
- **Example:** illustrate concept
- **Data:** support claim/hypothesis
- **Comparison:** show differences/similarities
- **Detail:** clarify complex idea
- **Result:** demonstrate effectiveness

**Check context:**
- What point is being made in that paragraph?
- How does the information support that point?

**⚠️ Common Traps in Teks Saintek**

---

**TRAP #1: The "Technical Overwhelm" Trap**

❌ **Panic reaction:**
"Banyak banget istilah yang gak ngerti! Pasti gak bisa jawab!"

✅ **Correct approach:**
- **Don't panic!** You don't need to understand EVERY term
- Focus on **overall concept**
- Technical terms usually **explained in context**
- Questions often test **understanding**, not memorization

**Example:**
You don't need to know what "mitochondria" is if the text says:
> *"Mitochondria, the powerhouse of the cell that produces energy..."*

The definition is **right there**!

---

**TRAP #2: The "Missing the Main Point" Trap**

❌ **Getting lost in details:**
Fokus pada specific data/numbers, miss main concept

✅ **Stay big picture:**
- Details **support** main idea, they're not THE main idea
- Ask: "What's the OVERALL concept being explained?"
- Main idea = **what the WHOLE passage is about**, not one paragraph

**Example:**
Text about climate change with lots of CO₂ data:
- Main idea: **NOT** "CO₂ levels are 400 ppm"
- Main idea: **Climate change is caused by greenhouse gas emissions**

---

**TRAP #3: The "Cause-Effect Reversal" Trap**

❌ **Confusing direction:**
Saying A causes B when actually B causes A

✅ **Track carefully:**
- **Pay attention to signal words**
- "X **causes** Y" ≠ "Y **causes** X"
- "X **results from** Y" = Y causes X

**Example:**
"Increased exercise **leads to** improved health"
- Cause: increased exercise
- Effect: improved health
- **NOT** the other way around!

---

**TRAP #4: The "Over-Inference" Trap**

❌ **Adding information:**
Concluding things **not stated** or **not supported** by data

✅ **Stick to text:**
- Science questions want **what's actually stated**
- Don't bring in outside knowledge (unless clearly a general science fact)
- If data shows correlation, don't assume causation (unless text says so)

**Example:**
Text: "Group A scored higher than Group B"
❌ Wrong inference: "Group A is more intelligent"
✅ What's supported: "Group A performed better on this test"

---

**TRAP #5: The "Partial Information" Trap**

❌ **Incomplete answer:**
Choosing option with only PART of the correct information

✅ **Complete picture:**
- Make sure answer includes **all key elements**
- Check if there are **multiple factors** mentioned
- Don't pick "half-right" answers

**Example:**
Question: "What factors contribute to obesity?"
Text mentions: diet, exercise, genetics
❌ Partial: "Poor diet"
✅ Complete: "Poor diet, lack of exercise, and genetic factors"

---

**TRAP #6: The "Ignoring Hedging Language" Trap**

Science uses **qualifying language**:
- "may", "might", "could", "possibly", "suggests", "appears to"

❌ **Missing qualifiers:**
Treating tentative findings as absolute facts

✅ **Note uncertainty:**
- "The study suggests..." ≠ "The study proves..."
- "May contribute" ≠ "Definitely causes"

**In answers:**
- Options with absolute language (always, never, all, none) often **wrong**
- Options with hedging (may, can, some, often) often **correct**

---

**TRAP #7: The "Unit Confusion" Trap**

❌ **Misreading measurements:**
Confusing mm with cm, mg with g, etc.

✅ **Check units carefully:**
- 1000 mg = 1 g (not same thing!)
- 100 cm = 1 m
- Pay attention to prefixes (kilo-, milli-, micro-)

**💡 Pro Reading Tips for Teks Saintek**

---

**TIP #1: Master the Structure First**

**Before diving into details:**
1. Identify structure (cause-effect? process? problem-solution?)
2. Locate key sections (introduction, methods, results, conclusion)
3. Understand flow

**Why?**
- Helps you **predict** where to find information
- Makes **scanning** more efficient
- Reduces **overwhelm**

---

**TIP #2: Create Mental Diagrams**

**For processes:**
Visualize as flowchart:
```
Step 1 → Step 2 → Step 3 → Result
```

**For cause-effect:**
```
Cause A ──→ Effect B ──→ Effect C
```

**For classification:**
```
Main Category
├─ Type 1
├─ Type 2
└─ Type 3
```

**Why?**
- Visual organization **boosts recall**
- Makes relationships **clearer**
- Easier to answer sequence/structure questions

---

**TIP #3: Focus on Topic Sentences**

**In scientific texts:**
- First sentence of each paragraph **usually** states main point
- Rest of paragraph provides **details/evidence**

**Strategy:**
1. Read all first sentences first (quick overview)
2. Understand overall flow
3. Dive into details as needed for questions

**Time-saver!**

---

**TIP #4: Don't Get Stuck on Unknown Terms**

**When you hit difficult vocabulary:**

**Step 1:** Keep reading!
- Often explained in next sentence
- Or you can understand from context

**Step 2:** Look for clues:
- Definitions in commas
- "Is" statements
- Examples given

**Step 3:** Break down the word:
- Familiar roots? (photo, bio, geo)
- Prefix meaning? (anti-, pre-, post-)
- Suffix hint? (-tion, -ity, -ous)

**Step 4:** If still unclear AND not critical for question:
- **Skip it!** Not worth getting stuck
- Focus on understandable parts

---

**TIP #5: Pay Extra Attention to Data**

**Numbers stand out** = likely important

**When you see data:**
- **Highlight mentally** (or mark if allowed)
- Note **what's being measured**
- Understand **comparison** (bigger? smaller? same?)
- Remember **approximate values** (exact numbers often not needed)

**Questions often test:**
- Highest/lowest values
- Comparisons between groups
- Trends (increase/decrease)

---

**TIP #6: Trace Cause-Effect Chains**

**Science is full of chain reactions:**

**Example:**
- ↑ CO₂ → ↑ greenhouse effect → ↑ temperature → ice melting → sea level rise

**Strategy:**
- Map the chain as you read
- Understand **each link**
- Can answer questions about:
  - What causes X? (trace backward)
  - What results from Y? (trace forward)
  - Why does Z occur? (follow the chain)

---

**TIP #7: Separate Facts from Interpretations**

**Facts:**
- "The temperature was 25°C" ✓
- "75% of subjects improved" ✓

**Interpretations:**
- "This suggests that..." ← conclusion drawn
- "This may indicate..." ← inference
- "Scientists believe..." ← opinion (though expert)

**Why distinguish?**
- Different confidence levels
- "According to the passage" vs "It can be inferred"

---

**TIP #8: Use Process of Elimination**

**For technical questions:**

❌ Eliminate:
- Options with information **not mentioned**
- Options **contradicting** the text
- Options that are **too extreme** (always, never, only)
- Options confusing **cause with effect**

✅ Look for:
- Options matching **text phrasing** (paraphrased)
- Options with **qualifying language** (may, can, often)
- Options that are **complete** (include all factors mentioned)

---

**🎯 Quick Checklist: "Is This Teks Saintek?"**

✅ Banyak technical terminology  
✅ Objective, impersonal tone  
✅ Contains data/measurements/statistics  
✅ Explains processes, concepts, or research  
✅ Uses precise, specific language  
✅ Passive voice common  
✅ Focus on facts, not emotions  
✅ Logical, structured organization  

Kalau mostly YES → It's Teks Saintek!
